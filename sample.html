25 locksmith listings, Lyon — the dossier, with every name masked

This is the real sample behind the figures on the sales page: 25 listings read on public Google Maps pages on 23 November 2026, between 10:26 and 10:52 in the morning, without a Google account. Company names, street numbers, street names and phone numbers are replaced here by masks. What you see is the form of the deliverable: what is measured, how it is scored, and what an evidence sheet looks like.

Read this first. A high score is not a conviction. Two listings can share an address because they share a real workshop; a name can be duplicated by a second branch. The dossier reports facts visible to any visitor and quotes Google’s own rules — the ruling belongs to Google. Nothing here is sent to anyone: the buyer is the only recipient.

Google rule quoted, word for word (source: support.google.com/business/answer/3038177):

“There should only be one listing per business location. Otherwise, your information might not display correctly on Google Maps and in Google Search.”

Second rule quoted, on the name:

“You should not include unnecessary information in your business name.”

The complaint text, provided ready to paste — written in the buyer’s name, to be filed by the buyer from their own account:

I am a locksmith in [your city] and I trade from [your workshop address].
I am reporting a listing which, on the public information available,
does not appear to represent a real business at that address.

Listing: [exact displayed name], [displayed address], [displayed phone], [link]

Public facts observed:
1. The same street address is declared by another business listing:
   [other listing], [address], [phone]. Two separate businesses cannot
   occupy one address without one of them being inaccurate.
2. The displayed name contains information that is not the business
   name ([trade], [city], [district]): “[displayed name]”.
3. The displayed number is a mobile number, unrelated to a workshop address.

These facts conflict, in my view, with the following rules, quoted from
the official page “Guidelines for representing your business on Google”:
 — “There should only be one listing per business location.”
 — “You should not include unnecessary information in your business name.”
 — “Make sure your address and/or service area are accurate and precise.”

I am not asking for a competitor to be removed: I am asking for the
existence of this business at this address to be verified.

[your name, role, phone, link to your own listing]

What this sheet does not do: it does not accuse anyone of fraud, it does not mention reviews, and it does not ask for any sanction. It quotes public facts and Google’s own rules — which is what makes it worth reading on Google’s side.

3 · What the sample does not contain

Two fields that other audits like to sell are absent, because they cannot be measured honestly from public pages:

  • Number of photos — Google shows no usable counter; the header says “Photos & videos” without a total.
  • Date of the first review — the public review list no longer offers an oldest-first sort (23/11/2026: “most relevant”, “newest”, “highest”, “lowest”). Reaching the first review means scrolling the whole list: 6 screens for 56 reviews, 38 screens for 375.

Those two rules are therefore not part of the delivered ranking, and never appear as a line item. A dossier claiming them would be selling an opinion as a measurement.

4 · What was not done

No complaint filed. No message sent to any business. No Google account used or created. No paid data source. No payment.
